Can outsiders verify what was installed?
The Opus 4.5 case from intro pillar (i) is the verification gap in concrete form. Anthropic deployed the
model on 24 November 2025 with no published constitution. Eight days later, a user noticed Claude
emitting soul overview in outputs and extracted an approximation. Amanda Askell confirmed
publicly that Claude was trained on the document. Anthropic released the official 23,000-word
version on 21 January 2026 — two months after deployment.
Even now, the lab does not publish direct evidence that the deployed model was actually trained on
the published constitution. And even if it was, further training (RLHF, deployment-time patches)
could cause the installed values to drift from the stated text.
The recoverability question splits into two sub-questions: did the model memorize its constitution,
so the text can be reproduced exactly or near-exactly from input-output behavior? And if not,
12
is the constitution that can be recovered behaviorally substantively the same as the stated one —
same content, different surface form?
Project 2 asks both, given only input-output access. The substantive output is whether an external
party can verify a stated constitution by recovering one from behavioral probes and comparing the
two — on the axes of exact-text recoverability and functional equivalence. A positive result on
either gives auditors, regulators, and adversarial users a portable verification method that works on
any model one can query, open-weight or API-only. A negative result identifies a structural gap:
stated values cannot be externally verified from behavior, and the trust assumption is irreducible.
Prior work
Open Character Training (Maiya et al., 2025) reports that character training “changes manner over
content” and that revealed-preference profiles converge across architectures (Spearman 0.44 → 0.87
on cross-model preferences after Loving training). Whether the constitution itself can be recovered
from those revealed preferences is the question they don’t ask.
Three adjacent lines of recovery research approach the constitution-as-artifact target without quite
reaching it.
Carlini et al. (2021). Recovers training data — including unique, privacy-sensitive examples
— from black-box LLM queries via membership-inference-style probing. The recovery target is
individual training examples; I extend the line from individual-example recovery to structured
value-artifact recovery. The methodological connection is direct: contrast articulation is a recovery
technique applied to a different artifact class.
Model-extraction work (Tram`er et al., 2016). Recovers behavior — typically a classifier’s
decision boundary or a generative model’s output distribution — from black-box queries. Project 2
sits closer to this line than to Carlini’s, because the recovery target is a behavioral specification (the
constitution determines comparative judgments, not memorized text). The constitution is a richer
target than a system prompt and a leaner one than full model behavior — a structured artifact
that mediates between the two.
Prompt-recovery work (Krishna et al., 2020). Recovers system prompts from outputs.
Constitution recovery extends this: a constitution is more than a system prompt because it operates
inside a structured judging pipeline.
The Bhatia et al. (2025) finding that values are installed at the SFT stage and barely shift through
DPO/PPO suggests the recoverable artifact, if it exists, is in the SFT-induced behavior. The open
question: when SFT installs a constitution, can I read it out — and under what conditions of
installation is it readable?
Predictions
A two-condition design tests the substance question. Both conditions train the same student model
on the same constitution C; they differ in how C is exposed during training.
13
Condition A — Standard OCT (without C-text training). Maiya pipeline as published. C
is in the teacher’s system prompt during DPO; the student internalizes C via DPO preferences only.
The student never sees C as a training document.
Condition B — model spec midtraining of C. The student is first trained on a corpus of
synthetic documents that discuss C and attribute model behavior to its principles, following Li
et al. (2026). Then standard OCT proceeds on top. Synthetic-document midtraining is the right
operationalization rather than raw-text SFT for two reasons: it is the published recipe shown to
instill recoverable spec content (the MSM ablation finds that explicitly attributing preferences to
the value matters; merely co-mentioning does not), and raw-text SFT on the literal C document
tends to over-fit surface phrasings and is closer to memorisation than to installation.
Installation gap. Recovery is much easier in Condition B than in Condition A. In Condition
B, the recovery success rate on a strong recovery method approaches 100% on the constitution’s
content, since midtraining exposed the student to attribution-rich documents about C that the
model can then articulate. In Condition A, the recovery success rate is variable and depends on
installation faithfulness. The installation gap quantifies the substance question: what does behavioral
OCT installation add over prior textual exposure to the constitution’s content? The gap is large if
behavioral installation produces a behaviorally-equivalent but textually-distinct artifact; small if
behavioral installation leaves the constitution as recoverable as midtraining exposure does.

Free-form probing produces narrative-coherence-inflated outputs (the model tells a clean story
even if the underlying installation is messy); contrast articulation forces the model to ground its
description in observable behavioral differences.

Library-dependence. A criterion not in {cj} cannot be recovered, so constraint-based is a strong
attribution method (criterion-by-criterion, prose-free, immune to introspection failures) but a
discovery method only to the extent the library includes plausible non-C criteria. Anchor-set
inclusion gives breadth; decoy inclusion guards against overfit. The complementarity with contrast
articulation is direct: contrast articulation can in principle surface novel criteria via prose but is
introspection-bounded; constraint-based reads behavior directly but is library-bounded.
Articulation-vs-elicitation taxonomy. Four levels of recovery, from strongest to weakest:
• Direct elicitation success: model quotes or paraphrases C closely.
• Articulation without elicitation: model can describe its values in C-aligned terms but can’t
reconstruct the document.
• Behavioral evidence without articulation: model behaves C-aligned but can’t articulate;
constraint-based recovery (above) carries the load — its prose-free regression on response-pair
forced choices is what handles this cell.
• No recovery: behavioral-only with no recovery from any method.
Expected distributions per condition: Condition B should be heavy on articulation without elicitation
(the midtraining stage gives the model explicit content to describe); Condition A should split among
the other modes.

Scope. The methodological contribution — recovery method development, validation against
ground-truth installed constitutions, failure-mode taxonomy — is established on small open-weight
calibration models, where the installed constitution is known and recovery accuracy can be measured.
Frontier application (Claude, GPT, Gemini) is included as a descriptive arm with bounded interpretation. The primary confound is the cheaper-variant baseline used in contrast articulation (see
Baseline family above): Flash and mini differ from the frontier sibling in scale, RLHF recipe, and
stylistic register, so frontier recoveries conflate constitutional values with non-constitutional factors.
The substantive recovery validation lives in the calibration arm. A secondary confound applies to
models with publicly-available constitutions (Claude, GPT): recovery is confounded by post-hoc text
memorization (a recovered C
′ aligning with public text is consistent with both training-as-stated
and memorization of leaked text), and disambiguation depends on calibration-signature matching
(Condition A vs. Condition B) plus within-model contrast-articulation-vs-free-form attributability.
Models without published constitutions (Gemini) admit cleaner recovery tests on the memorization
axis; failure-to-recover findings on those are memorization-confound-immune.
N and replication
3 recovery attempts per method per model; report mean ± 95% CI. Total runs: 5 methods × 3
attempts × ∼6 model populations (3 calibration + 3 application) = 90 runs. Cheap relative to the
rest of the program.
Failure modes and success
Primary failure mode: hallucinated recovery. The model’s recovered constitution C
′
could
be more about what the model thinks the prober wants to hear (or what the model thinks a
typical OCT-trained model is told) than about what was actually installed. The bloated-criterion
failure mode is the diagnostic signature, but distinguishing genuine emergent realignment from
prober-anchoring requires care — both can produce bloated recoveries.
How I address it: contrast articulation as primary recovery method tests attributability. The model
is asked to describe what differs between itself and a non-C-trained baseline; this forces the recovered
description to be grounded in observable behavioral differences, not in narrative priors. Free-form
probing as baseline — if free-form gives a clean-narrative recovery and contrast articulation gives a
less-clean one, the difference is the narrative-coherence inflation. The Condition B → Condition A
gap is the substance test. If the gap is large (B near 100%, A variable), the recovery method is
sensitive to actual installation. If the gap is small (both produce similar recoveries), the recovery is
hallucinated regardless of installation.
Secondary failure mode: CEI numerical instability. The Constitution Equivalence Index
depends on ridge-regression R2 values; with small calibration sets, R2
estimates are noisy and the
CEI threshold for failure-mode classification (CEI > 0.9 for faithful, etc.) is unstable near the
boundary. N = 3 recovery attempts per method per model with mean ± 95% CI on CEI; require
statistical significance against the threshold before classifying. CKA fallback if classification flips
between recovery attempts on the same model. The bounded re-OCT validation (e) on 2–3 selected
interesting cases serves as ground-truth check on the CEI classification.
